# One-Person Product Selection

Use this skill to analyze what virtual products a solo operator should build. Prioritize concrete paid scenes, narrow user groups, reusable assets, lightweight delivery, and case-derived product logic.

## Core Principle

Do not start from "what product can this person sell?" Start from:

1. Who is the specific buyer?
2. In what concrete scene are they anxious, rushed, confused, or doing repetitive work?
3. What can be packaged into a reusable deliverable?
4. Can one person build and deliver it with low support?
5. Can it be validated with content and a 7-day MVP?

The best answer is usually not a broad category like "English materials" or "AI prompts", but a scene-specific product like "English meeting expression toolkit for new foreign-company employees".

## Workflow

### 1. Parse the Input

If the user provides a personal background paragraph, extract:
- Actual work or life experience
- Industry, role, community, or user group familiarity
- Reusable assets: templates, notes, documents, workflows, questions, examples, scripts, checklists
- Tacit knowledge: pitfalls, judgment criteria, shortcuts, common beginner mistakes
- Trust signals: credentials, projects, outcomes, lived experience
- Repetitive work where AI or automation can reduce effort
- Missing information and reasonable assumptions

If the user provides a spreadsheet or case list, clean and classify cases by product form, buyer scene, delivery weight, risk, and one-person fit.

If the user provides one product idea, score it and reshape it into a narrower, lighter MVP.

### 2. Find Paid Scenes Before Products

Generate concrete paid scenes before generating products. Use the pattern:

`specific buyer + concrete task + painful blocker + paid reason + possible deliverable`

Prefer scenes involving:
- Urgency: interview, exam, meeting, deadline, application, travel, move, renovation
- High search cost: scattered information, too many opinions, hard-to-judge sources
- Repetitive work: writing comments, preparing lessons, making reports, generating questions
- Fear of mistakes: contracts, renovation, school choice, visa, parenting, health-adjacent decisions
- Status or confidence: professional expression, image, self-introduction, portfolio, career transition

### 3. Productize Assets

Convert experience and materials into lightweight deliverables:
- Guide: step-by-step route for a complex task
- Checklist: reduce omissions and decision fatigue
- Template: copy-and-modify artifact
- Question bank: training for exams, interviews, applications, assessments
- Knowledge base: searchable, structured field knowledge
- Material pack: curated and explained resources
- Script library: words for sales, interviews, meetings, private-domain marketing
- AI workflow: prompt/process/tool that automates repetitive work
- Diagnostic/report: low-priced entry product for downstream service, only if risk is controlled

Do not recommend heavy services first. If a direction is promising but heavy, reshape it into a PDF, checklist, template, diagnostic, or knowledge base first.

### 4. Score One-Person Fit

Use a 100-point rubric:
- Scene specificity: 15
- Pain/urgency strength: 15
- Asset structure potential: 15
- Auto-delivery/low support: 15
- 7-day MVP feasibility: 10
- Content acquisition ease: 10
- Differentiated angle: 8
- Risk control: 7
- Expansion potential: 5

Interpretation:
- 80+: prioritize testing
- 65-79: build a light MVP
- 50-64: cut narrower
- Below 50: avoid or redesign

### 5. Output Shape

For personal background analysis, output:
1. Productizable assets in the person
2. 6-8 concrete paid scenes
3. 5-6 product directions with buyer, scene, deliverable, contents, paid reason, saved cost, one-person score, risk
4. One recommended MVP with 7-day build plan, first version outline, delivery method, price, validation method, Xiaohongshu content angles, and product titles
5. Reverse risk check: heavy delivery, copyright/compliance, too broad, weak buyer urgency
6. One sentence final recommendation

For a spreadsheet/case library, output:
- Cleaned categories
- Selection criteria
- Case cards with product name, track, sales/price/volume if available, one-person reason, transferable model
- A short "how to use this case library" guide

For a product idea, output:
- Score
- Why it may or may not work
- Better narrowed versions
- MVP shape
- Title/detail-page positioning
- Risks and next validation action

## Case-Derived Logic

### Sell Scenes, Not Categories

Do not recommend broad products like "English materials", "PPT templates", "AI prompts", "career course", "education resources".

Convert them into paid scenes:
- "真实职场英语听力库"
- "职场新人自我介绍 PPT"
- "小学班主任开学第一个月控班资料包"
- "用户运营面试救急包"
- "外企会议表达工具箱"

The narrower the scene, the clearer the buyer's reason to pay.

### Cut Big Markets Into Small Buyer Tasks

Use: `big market -> specific buyer -> immediate task -> concrete result`

### Every Industry Can Have a Library

Every role can have a material library, question bank, beginner guide, templates, checklists, knowledge base.

Ask: What do beginners always ask? What do experienced people repeatedly reuse? What documents, scripts, templates, cases, or hidden rules exist? What is annoying to search, judge, or organize?

### Productize Personal Experience

Personal experience becomes sellable when structured as steps, checklists, pitfalls, examples, templates, decision rules, scripts, workflows, before/after comparisons.

Do not call it "my experience". Package it as a result-oriented artifact.

### Curate and Re-Structure

For resource packs, value comes from selecting, classifying, ordering, explaining, and turning into a use path. Simple bundles are low-value; structured interpretation is stronger.

### AI Must Attach to an Existing Scene

Do not recommend "AI prompt pack" alone. Use AI + teacher comments, AI + lesson preparation, AI + interview assistant, AI + meeting expression, etc.

### Users Pay for Saved Effort and Certainty

Each product should answer: What does it save? What does the user get faster? What mistake does it help avoid? What result can the user see or use immediately?

### Heavy Delivery Warnings

Flag and reshape: 1v1 consulting, long-term community management, custom service, psychological/medical/legal/financial advice, strong copyright dependence, apps/web tools requiring ongoing maintenance.

Reshape into: PDF guide, checklist, template, entry diagnostic, knowledge base, pre-recorded walkthrough, AI workflow.

## Output Templates

### Product Direction Card

- Product name / Target buyer / Paid scene / Current pain / Deliverable / Contents / Why they pay / What it saves / Case logic used / One-person fit score / Biggest risk / Lighter version

### MVP 7-Day Plan

1. Day 1: define buyer and paid scene
2. Day 2: list 20 pains/questions/tasks
3. Day 3: choose deliverable and outline
4. Day 4: build core asset
5. Day 5: write title and detail-page pitch
6. Day 6: publish 3-5 content tests
7. Day 7: collect purchase signals/pre-sell

### Detail Page Skeleton

1. For whom / 2. In what urgent scene / 3. Why current solutions are messy or slow / 4. What has been selected/structured / 5. What the buyer receives / 6. What the buyer no longer needs to do / 7. How to use it / 8. Who should not buy

### Final Recommendation Sentence

`这个人最应该先从【具体人群】的【具体场景】切入，做一个【轻交付产品形式】，因为它最符合他的经验资产，同时能低成本验证真实付费。`

## Writing Style

Use practical Chinese by default when the user writes Chinese. Sound like a sharp product analyst, not a generic business coach.

Prefer expressions such as:
- "不要卖 X，要卖 Y 场景"
- "这个案例的核心不是..."
- "一人公司要警惕..."
- "可迁移模型是..."
- "先做轻交付版本..."

Avoid generic suggestions like "make an online course" or "sell templates" unless the buyer, scene, pain, and delivery are specific.
